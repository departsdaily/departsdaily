THE BRIEFING — MIAMI STRAIGHT UP

South Beach is the least interesting part of Miami, and it absorbs most of the visitors. The Art Deco blocks are worth an hour of walking and the sand is genuinely good, but the city that people move here for is on the mainland, in Spanish, and mostly out of sight of the ocean. Cross the causeway on day one and the trip changes shape. Spanish is the working language across most of the mainland, and nobody will think less of you for pointing at what you want.

A few things are true here and nowhere else in America. Miami is the only major U.S. city with two national parks on its doorstep — the Everglades to the west and Biscayne to the south, and Biscayne is roughly ninety-five percent water. The whole place is built on porous limestone barely above sea level, which is why there are no basements and why a hard rain floods a street in ten minutes. And it is credited as the only major American city founded at the push of a woman, Julia Tuttle, who talked a railroad into extending south in the 1890s.

It suits people who want heat, water and a city that eats late — dinner at nine is normal, and the night does not really start until later. It suits anyone curious enough to order in a second language. It does not suit people who want to walk everywhere: the neighborhoods are real but they are far apart, and outside a few corridors you are in a car. It also does not suit anyone allergic to being upsold — valet, bottle service, and the beach chair are all a transaction here.

WHERE YOU'D ACTUALLY BE STANDING

WYNWOOD

A converted warehouse district north of downtown where the blank walls are painted and the old loading bays are galleries and taprooms. It is walkable by Miami standards and the stronger pick if nightlife matters more to you than sand. The tradeoff is obvious — no beach at all, and the edges of the district are still industrial and empty after dark.

CORAL GABLES

A planned 1920s Mediterranean-revival town of coral-rock arches and banyan-shaded streets, including a public pool carved out of an old quarry. Quiet, handsome, and strictly zoned down to the sign fonts. The tradeoff is that it is a residential city with an early bedtime, and the beach is a twenty-five-minute drive.

COCONUT GROVE

The oldest continuously settled neighborhood in Miami, on the bay, under heavy tree canopy, with marinas and low-key sidewalk cafes. It is the calmest good option and works well for families. The tradeoff is that it feels suburban, and swimming means driving — the bay here is for boats, not bathers.

BRICKELL

The high-rise financial canyon just south of the Miami River, with rooftop pools, dense restaurants and the free elevated Metromover looping through it. Central, walkable, and strong midweek value when the bankers go home. The tradeoff is the setting — glass towers and a highway, with the beach a bridge and a bottleneck away.

WHEN TO GO — AND WHEN NOT TO

September through November is when the fares and the hotel rates fall, and it is worth knowing exactly why. Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30 and peaks from mid-August into early October, so September is cheap because it carries risk. If you go then, book refundable and cover the trip. Summer generally is hot, humid and punctuated by an afternoon thunderstorm that arrives on schedule and passes in an hour.

December through April is the dry season, the weather is close to flawless, and the whole city prices like it knows. Specific spikes: the art fair in early December, when Miami Beach rates go somewhere unreasonable, the wine-and-food festival in February, spring break through March, the tennis tournament and the electronic music festival that land in the same late-March stretch, and the Formula 1 race weekend in May. Any of those will double a room.

ONE THING TO SKIP

SKIP SOUTH BEACH IN MARCH. Spring break turns those twenty blocks into a crowd-control operation. In recent years the city has responded with curfews, restricted causeway access at night, extra police and early bar closures — and the hotels charge peak rates for the privilege of being in the middle of it. The sand is packed, the traffic is stopped, and the thing you came for is the one thing you cannot get near. If your dates are locked into March, sleep on the mainland or up in Mid-Beach and visit South Beach in the morning, when it is briefly the neighborhood it is supposed to be.
