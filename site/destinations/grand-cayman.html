THE BRIEFING — GRAND CAYMAN STRAIGHT UP

Grand Cayman is the Caribbean island for people who do not actually want an adventure, and that is a compliment. The roads are good, the water is drinkable, the hospital is real, the crime rate is low, and nothing about arriving requires improvisation. If your idea of a holiday is figuring out a chaotic minibus system in a language you half speak, this is not your island. If it is snorkeling before breakfast and a proper dinner without a plan B, it very much is.

Two facts are worth knowing before you look at any price. The Cayman Islands dollar is pegged above the US dollar, so a number on a menu is meaningfully larger than the same number at home, and it catches almost everyone once. And this is a British Overseas Territory, which means you drive on the left, through roundabouts, on the main road along the beach. There is also no river anywhere on the island — the whole thing is porous limestone, which is exactly why the water offshore is that colour and why visibility over the reef is so absurd.

It suits divers above all — the wall drops off close to shore and the operators are as professional as anywhere in the region. It suits families, cautious first-time Caribbean travellers, and anyone who wants a beach that does not require a strategy. It does not suit budget travellers without a plan; this is a financial centre with financial-centre prices, and the difference between a trip here that stings and one that does not is almost entirely about how many meals you cook.

WHERE YOU'D ACTUALLY BE STANDING

SEVEN MILE BEACH, NORTH END

The strip is closer to five and a half miles than seven, and it is not uniform — the northern half has the widest sand, the calmest water, and the shortest walks between resorts and restaurants. Staying up here means you can eat somewhere different every night on foot. It is the most expensive stretch of the island and it never feels remote.

WEST BAY

Past the north end of the beach, where the resorts thin out into a residential district with its own quiet shore. Cheaper beds, local grocery stores, and a much better sense of what living here looks like. You will drive or take the bus to dinner most nights, which some people count as freedom and others as a chore.

GEORGE TOWN AND SOUTH SOUND

The capital is the cruise port and the office district, busy by day and largely closed by evening, with quieter waterfront homes running east along South Sound. Good value and close to the airport. The beach here is rocky rather than sandy, so you are commuting to the sand.

RUM POINT AND CAYMAN KAI

Across the North Sound at the top of the island, an hour from the airport by road, all shallow turquoise water and casuarina trees. This is where you go to see nobody. The tradeoff is total dependence on a car and a very short list of places to eat after dark.

WHEN TO GO — AND WHEN NOT TO

Mid-May into June and again in September and October are the cheap months, and September is the cheapest for the obvious reason — that is the peak of hurricane season, which runs June through November. Cayman gets hot and humid through those months but the sea is at its warmest and the dive boats are half full. Buy the flexible fare and watch the forecast rather than avoiding the season outright.

December through April is dry, breezy, and priced like it. Christmas and New Year are the hardest weeks of the year to book at any price. Three recurring events tighten things further: a food and wine weekend in January that fills the Seven Mile resorts, Batabano carnival in early May, and the national Pirates Week festival in November. Also check the cruise schedule — on heavy ship days George Town and the popular snorkel sites are a different experience entirely.

ONE THING TO SKIP

SKIP THE DAY-ONE RENTAL CAR. Do not collect a car the hour you land. You will be driving on the left for the first time, in an unfamiliar vehicle, through a chain of roundabouts on the busiest road on the island, after a travel day. Visitors also need a temporary local driving permit, which the rental agencies issue but which adds a step people do not expect at the counter. The bus runs the beach road constantly and taxis are priced by zone, so the first two days need no car at all. Rent for the one day you want to circle the island to the East End, then give it back.
