THE BRIEFING — SAN JUAN STRAIGHT UP

Puerto Rico is the easiest foreign-feeling trip an American can take, and calling it foreign is exactly the mistake. You board a domestic flight, walk off without a customs hall, spend dollars, and your phone works on the plan you already pay for — no passport, no exchange rate, no roaming bill, no border. Only the US Virgin Islands offer anything close, and nothing over there is a walled colonial capital. It is the reason a fare deal to SJU is worth more to you than the same number to any island a passport away.

Old San Juan is the part that exists nowhere else. The city was founded in 1521, which makes it the oldest European-founded city under the US flag, and the blue-grey cobbles underfoot are adoquines — slag blocks that arrived as ballast in Spanish ships and were laid down as pavement. The old town is still ringed by its defensive walls, still bookended by two forts on the headland, and you can walk the entire circuit in an afternoon with the Atlantic on one side and painted eighteenth-century facades on the other.

It suits travellers who want a real city and a real beach in the same trip and are happy to move between them — heat, hills, late dinners, salsa coming out of a doorway at eleven at night. It suits solo travellers and first-time Caribbean visitors because the safety net of a US territory is under you the whole time. It does not suit anyone who wants the all-inclusive bubble where every meal and drink is pre-paid and nothing is decided. That is a different island and a different flight.

WHERE YOU'D ACTUALLY BE STANDING

OLD SAN JUAN

Seven walkable blocks of colour stacked on a hill between El Morro and San Cristobal, with the cruise piers at the bottom of it. Stay here if you want history out the door and dinner two minutes away on foot. The tradeoff is real: no swimming beach, midday crowds when several ships are docked, and rooms in old buildings that are charming rather than large.

CONDADO

The beachfront strip ten minutes east — high-rises, surf out front, a lagoon behind, restaurants along Ashford Avenue. It is the safe first-timer choice if you want sand and a proper dinner without arranging transport. The tradeoff is that it is the most expensive sand in the metro and it reads more like Miami Beach than like Puerto Rico.

SANTURCE

Inland between Condado and the old city, this is where the murals and the nightlife are. La Placita is a working produce market by day whose surrounding square turns into an open-air bar scene on weekend nights. Fits people who want to eat and drink where locals do; not for anyone who wants to swim before breakfast.

ISLA VERDE

Minutes from SJU, technically in Carolina rather than San Juan, with the longest stretch of good sand in the metro area and the resort towers that go with it. Ideal for a short trip or an early flight home. The tradeoff is that everything with character is a drive away, and you will make that drive daily.

WHEN TO GO — AND WHEN NOT TO

Late August through November is when the fares fall, and the reason is hurricane season. The Atlantic season officially runs June 1 to November 30 and concentrates hard between mid-August and mid-October. That does not mean a storm hits your week — most weeks are fine, hot, and half-price — but it means you book refundable or insured and you watch the forecast rather than pretending it does not exist. Mid-December through April is peak, dry, breezy, and priced accordingly.

The spikes are worth knowing. Mid-January brings the Fiestas de la Calle San Sebastian, which fills the old city and every room near it. The island also keeps the longest Christmas season in the Americas, running from late November through Three Kings Day on January 6 and the octavitas after it, so rates hold firm through the whole stretch. Easter week fills the beaches with local families. Summer is hot and wet, but the rain tends to come in short afternoon bursts rather than grey all-day drizzle.

ONE THING TO SKIP

SKIP THE FULL-WEEK RENTAL CAR. Renting for the whole trip is the standard San Juan money leak. Old San Juan is narrow one-way cobblestone streets with almost no street parking, so you end up in a garage that fills by mid-morning, and Condado hotels charge for parking every single night you leave the car sitting there. Meanwhile taxis and rideshare cover the entire metro area cheaply and the airport run is under twenty minutes. Rent for the two days you are actually driving out — El Yunque, Fajardo, the west coast — and hand it back. You will pay less and you will stop hunting for spaces.
