KNOW BEFORE YOU LAND — THE SHAPE OF THE CITY

The airport is not in Detroit. DTW sits in Romulus, 20 miles southwest of downtown, and there is no train. Budget a rideshare (~$45–60) or grab the car up front — see Getting Around below for when each makes sense.
Downtown, Midtown, Corktown. Downtown is the towers, stadiums, and the People Mover loop. Midtown, up Woodward on the QLine streetcar, holds the DIA and the museums. Corktown, ten minutes west, is the 1830s Irish neighborhood where Ford spent six years restoring Michigan Central Station — a 1913 rail palace reopened in 2024 that is worth walking to just to stand in.
Skip the ruin tour. A decade ago every visitor came to photograph abandonment. The city finds it tiresome, half the famous ruins are renovated or gone, and you'd be paying to gawk at someone's block. Spend the same afternoon at Eastern Market's murals instead.

DO IT CHEAP — THE PLAYBOOK

Pick a side in the coney war. Lafayette and American Coney Island have sold chili dogs from adjoining storefronts on Lafayette Blvd for a century. Under $6, open late, and locals will demand to know which one you chose.
Detroit-style square at the source. Buddy's on Conant Street has baked the original thick square in blue-steel automotive pans since 1946 — crispy cheese edge, sauce on top. One pizza feeds two.
Belle Isle for the price of nothing. The island state park in the Detroit River has the country's oldest aquarium (1904) and a glass conservatory. Free on foot or bike; cars need a Michigan Recreation Passport.
Free Art Deco. The Guardian Building's tiled 1929 lobby — locals call it the Cathedral of Finance — costs nothing to walk into on a weekday. Look up.
Saturday means Eastern Market. The sheds have run since 1891; the surrounding blocks now carry one of the country's densest mural collections. Go hungry, leave with produce you didn't plan on.
