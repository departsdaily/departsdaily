THE BRIEFING — LAS VEGAS STRAIGHT UP

The Strip is not a walk, and getting that wrong is the most common first-timer mistake in Las Vegas. The resorts are so large that the sidewalk from one front door to its immediate neighbour can take fifteen or twenty minutes, crossing the road means climbing stairs to a pedestrian bridge and doubling back, and there is essentially no shade anywhere along it. People plan an ambitious day on foot in July and finish it beaten, in a rideshare, having seen three casinos.

The fact that explains most of the confusion: the Strip is not in Las Vegas. Those famous resorts sit in unincorporated Clark County, in the townships of Paradise and Winchester, outside the city limits entirely. That is why downtown Las Vegas is a separate place a few miles north — lower ceilings, older casinos, smaller table minimums, the Fremont Street canopy overhead. Two different cities sold under one name, and choosing between them is the single biggest decision you will make about the trip.

Vegas suits travellers who decide in advance what they came for — one show, one meal, one flight over the canyon — and then let the rest be free spectacle. It suits budget travellers better than almost any large US city, because rooms are priced to get you through a casino floor rather than to cover their cost. It does not suit people who need quiet, and it is a poor fit for young families: Nevada law keeps minors from lingering in gaming areas, and the gaming areas are what you walk through to reach everything else.

WHERE YOU'D ACTUALLY BE STANDING

DOWNTOWN AND FREMONT EAST

The original casinos packed under the light canopy, with the lowest table minimums in the city and drinks to match. Fremont East, past the canopy, is the bar district where people who live here go out. Fits gamblers and anyone chasing value. Tradeoff: rideshare each way to the Strip, and the blocks around it get rough quickly.

SOUTH STRIP

The Mandalay Bay and Luxor end, closest to the airport — minutes from touchdown to check-in, which matters on a two-night trip. Rooms usually price below the middle. Tradeoff: you are at one end of a very long road, and the walk to the centre is not a walk you will make twice.

EAST OF THE STRIP

The Paradise Road and Convention Center corridor holds ordinary hotels with no resort fees, no casino to cross and no queue at the desk, a short walk from the back entrances of the big resorts. Fits people optimising money over atmosphere. Tradeoff: convention weeks fill it solid, and the walk in is service roads and parking structures.

SUMMERLIN AND RED ROCK

Twenty miles west against the escarpment — locals' casinos, actual neighbourhoods, and Red Rock Canyon at the edge of it, where the scenic drive uses timed entry during the busy months. Fits hikers and anyone bringing kids. Tradeoff: you need the rental car, and you are not on the Strip at all.

DO IT CHEAP — THE PLAYBOOK

Fly Sun–Thu. Weekend fares and hotel rates are 2–3× midweek.
Count the resort fee. A "$35 room" with a $50 resort fee is an $85 room.
Eat one block off-Strip. Chinatown on Spring Mountain Road has the best food in the city at a third of Strip prices.
Free shows first. Bellagio fountains, the Sphere exterior, Fremont Street — schedule the free spectacle, then decide what's worth paying for.

WHEN TO GO — AND WHEN NOT TO

Deep summer is the cheap season, and it earns it: July and August afternoons run past 105 degrees, and that is not a figure of speech in a place with no shade and a lot of concrete. The other soft window is the first three weeks of December, before the holiday. Midweek is the rule that beats every other rule — Sunday through Thursday, rooms and shows cost a fraction of Saturday. March through May and October through November give you comfortable weather at a fair price.

Check the convention and event calendar before you buy the fare, because a few weeks a year the whole city reprices. CES in early January books out effectively every room. The Formula 1 race in November closes streets and multiplies rates. The National Finals Rodeo in early December, EDC in May, New Year's Eve, Super Bowl weekend, March Madness and championship fight nights all do the same thing on a smaller scale. Move your dates a week and you can halve the trip.

ONE THING TO SKIP

SKIP THE BUFFET PILGRIMAGE. The all-you-can-eat era that built the city's reputation is largely over. A great many buffets closed for good in 2020 and never came back, and the survivors are now positioned as destination dining — you queue anyway, you pay what a proper restaurant dinner costs, and you eat one meal's worth of food while telling yourself you are winning. It is the one Vegas tradition that no longer does what people remember it doing. Put the same money into the single thing you will still be describing to people a year from now, whether that is the helicopter, the show or the canyon.
