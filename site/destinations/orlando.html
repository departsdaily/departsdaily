THE BRIEFING — ORLANDO STRAIGHT UP

Orlando is the one destination where the flight is the cheapest part of the trip, and that should invert how you plan it. A good Charlotte fare might save you a hundred dollars a head; the ticket and hotel decisions you make afterwards swing several times that per day. So take the cheap fare when the board shows it, then spend your real planning energy on ticket math and lodging, which is where an Orlando trip is actually won or lost.

Physically it is inland central Florida — flat, lake-scattered, an hour or so from either coast, with a purpose-built resort corridor spread across the southwest side of the metro. The scale is genuinely hard to picture until you are in it: Walt Disney World covers something close to 25,000 acres, a footprint roughly the size of San Francisco, which is why "we'll just pop over" never works. Universal opened a fourth park in 2025, the first change to that side of the equation in years, and it made multi-day and park-to-park ticket choices worth recalculating rather than copying from an old blog post.

It suits planners and families and rewards booking ahead relentlessly — entry, dining, transport. It does not suit improvisers. And from June through September the weather runs your day whether you like it or not: central Florida gets afternoon thunderstorms most days and Florida records more lightning than any other state, so outdoor rides and hotel pools close with no notice and reopen when the cell passes. Do the outdoor things in the morning. There is also a real city here — downtown and the older suburbs are twenty to forty minutes from the corridor, and most visitors never see either.

WHERE YOU'D ACTUALLY BE STANDING

INTERNATIONAL DRIVE

The tourist spine running between the convention centre and Universal — hotels at every price, an observation wheel, outlet shopping, and a rare stretch you can actually walk. Fits a Universal-first trip or a convention add-on. The tradeoff is traffic that seizes up at predictable hours and a strip with no character to speak of.

KISSIMMEE / US-192

The older motel-and-rental corridor south of the Disney property, and the cheapest beds within striking distance of any gate. It is also where the vacation-home rentals big families use are clustered. The tradeoff is that quality varies wildly block to block, you absolutely need a car, and you are fifteen to twenty-five minutes out.

CELEBRATION

The planned town Disney developed in the 1990s just off 192 — front porches, a lakeside main street, streets that look art-directed because they were. Fits people who want quiet and a short drive to the parks. The tradeoff is that it is small and the evenings are very calm; this is not a night-out base.

WINTER PARK

Brick streets, live oaks and Rollins College north of downtown, with Park Avenue's shops and a museum holding the Tiffany chapel interior built for the 1893 Chicago world's fair. Fits adults on a no-park day. The tradeoff is the distance — thirty to forty minutes from the resort corridor, so it works as a day, not a base.

WHEN TO GO — AND WHEN NOT TO

The cheapest window is late August through September: hot, wet, the peak of hurricane season, and the lowest crowds of the year with hotel rates to match. The other two dips are the weeks after New Year's into early February, and the first ten days or so of December before the holiday build. Hurricane season officially runs June 1 to November 30 and peaks from mid-August into October — Orlando is inland, so the practical risk is wind, rain and a closed park day rather than storm surge, but build in flexibility.

Expensive and uncomfortable at once: Christmas week through New Year's Day, when parks can reach capacity and stop admitting people entirely; March spring break running into Easter; Thanksgiving week; and mid-June to mid-August, when the heat index and the crowd both top out. Two seasonal events quietly fill otherwise ordinary autumn weekends — the Halloween event that runs from late summer into October, and the Epcot food festival that stretches across the same months. Go midweek in autumn and it is a different trip.

ONE THING TO SKIP

SKIP THE FREE-TICKET TIMESHARE PITCH. Along US-192, on International Drive, and at booths inside some hotel lobbies you will be offered free or nearly free park tickets in exchange for a ninety-minute presentation. The presentation is not ninety minutes. It runs most of a day, they often shuttle you there so leaving early is awkward, and the "no" has to be said several times before it takes. You will trade a park day — on a trip where a park day costs real money per person — for tickets worth less than the day you lost. Buy authorised multi-day tickets in advance, and treat any offer conditional on attending anything as a no.
