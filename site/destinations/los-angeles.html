THE BRIEFING — LOS ANGELES STRAIGHT UP

Come to Los Angeles for the mountains, not the ocean. The Pacific here runs cold all year — locals surf in wetsuits in August — and the beach is a good afternoon rather than a reason to fly. The reason to fly is that the city sits in a basin ringed by real mountains, and you can be on a ridge trail fifteen minutes after breakfast, looking down at the entire grid running out to the water. On a clear winter week you can hike in the morning and see snow on the peaks behind you from a beach parking lot the same afternoon.

The other reason is what people eat. Los Angeles has the country's only officially designated Thai Town, a few blocks of Hollywood Boulevard that runs on late-night noodle shops, and the same city has official Little Armenia and Historic Filipinotown districts within a short drive of it. Add Koreatown's 24-hour kitchens and the taquerias of Boyle Heights and the East Side, and the honest itinerary here is built around meals with driving in between, not the other way round. Plan the day around where you want to eat and the sightseeing arranges itself.

It suits people willing to plan by geography and commit to it — one side of town per day, no crisscrossing. It suits hikers, drivers, film people and anyone who likes a city that does not care whether you are impressed. It does not suit anyone hoping for a compact walkable trip, and it does not suit visitors expecting glamour on arrival: most of Los Angeles is low-rise, sun-bleached and functional, and the postcard version is about six blocks wide. Budget for the fact that rush hour runs from roughly 3pm to 7pm and gets worse when it rains.

WHERE YOU'D ACTUALLY BE STANDING

SILVER LAKE & LOS FELIZ

Hillside streets on the east side, connected in places by public staircases, with walkable strips of cafes and small shops and Griffith Park at the top of the hill. Good for a trip built around food and trails. The tradeoff is distance — you are forty-plus minutes from the sand, and street parking is a nightly negotiation.

KOREATOWN

Dense, mid-city, and sitting on top of two subway lines running under Wilshire, which makes it one of the few places here you can stay without a car. Food at 2am, spas, karaoke, and rates well under the west side. The tradeoff is that it is a working neighborhood, not a scenic one — there is no view and no beach walk.

CULVER CITY

A small brick downtown wrapped around old studio lots, with the light rail line that runs from Santa Monica to downtown stopping right there. It splits the difference between the coast and the centre better than anywhere else. The tradeoff is that it is quiet after dinner and still a real drive to Hollywood.

PASADENA

Craftsman bungalows and shaded streets under the San Gabriel Mountains, with a walkable old town of brick storefronts and the Rose Bowl in the arroyo below. Calm, leafy, and cheaper than the west side. The tradeoff is the corner it sits in — two freeways stand between you and everything else in the city.

WHEN TO GO — AND WHEN NOT TO

January through April is the value window and the weather is better than the calendar suggests: warm, clear days between the rain, and the hills briefly green. This is a dry-summer climate, so nearly all the year's rain falls between December and March. May and June bring the marine layer — grey mornings at the coast that may or may not burn off by two, sunny the whole time ten miles inland. It is not a reason to cancel, but book the beach hotel knowing it.

September and October are the hottest and clearest months, and also the season for Santa Ana winds and wildfire smoke. Price spikes cluster around awards season in late February and early March, which hits Hollywood and Beverly Hills hotels hardest, New Year in Pasadena for the parade and the bowl game, and the school holidays from late June through August. Plan around July and August of 2028 as well — the Olympics will not be a value window.

ONE THING TO SKIP

SKIP RODEO DRIVE. It is three blocks of luxury flagships with door staff, and unless you are buying, there is nothing to do but photograph a street sign. People burn half a day and a parking fee to stand somewhere they have already seen in a film, and they remember none of it. If you want free Los Angeles that actually lands, the Getty Center charges nothing to enter — you pay only to park — and it gives you Renaissance paintings, a hillside garden and a view down the basin to the ocean. Same afternoon, no doorman, and you leave with something.
