THE BRIEFING — MONTEGO BAY STRAIGHT UP

The strongest argument for Montego Bay is how easy it is to leave it. MBJ is Jamaica's western gateway and the town around it is pleasant enough, but almost everything you will still be describing a year later sits between thirty minutes and two hours down the coast road. Book the flight here, then plan the days outward — the coast road east toward Falmouth and Ocho Rios, or west toward Negril, is where the trip actually happens.

The town itself is really two towns. There is the tourist half along Gloucester Avenue — a walkable half-mile of beach clubs, craft stalls and duty-free shops with the sea on one side and the airport approach directly overhead. Then there is the working city downtown, built around Sam Sharpe Square, named for the Baptist deacon who led the 1831 Christmas Rebellion of enslaved Jamaicans; the uprising was crushed and Sharpe was hanged, and within three years Britain had moved to abolish slavery. It is the rare resort town where the central square is a genuine piece of world history, and most visitors never see it because the shuttle turns the other way out of the airport.

It suits people who want a short flight to warm, calm, reef-protected water and would rather not drive. It suits all-inclusive travelers, first-time Caribbean travelers, and anyone who wants a boat day and a waterfall day and no logistics. It does not suit people who want to wander a city on foot at night, and it does not suit anyone who takes offense at being sold to — on the strip and on the beach you will be offered goods, rides and tours constantly. A friendly, firm no thank you is completely normal here, and it works.

WHERE YOU'D ACTUALLY BE STANDING

THE HIP STRIP

Gloucester Avenue, ten minutes from the airport, where the small hotels, bars and public beaches line up along the water. Stay here if you want to walk to dinner and pay island prices rather than resort prices. The tradeoff is noise from both directions — the bars at night and the flight path most of the day.

ROSE HALL & IRONSHORE

The resort belt strung east along the coast road, all gated grounds, golf and private stretches of sand. This is where the all-inclusives are and it delivers exactly what it promises. The tradeoff is that you are effectively captive: nothing is walkable, and every meal off property becomes a taxi negotiation.

DOWNTOWN MONTEGO BAY

The working city — markets, patty shops, traffic and Sam Sharpe Square. Worth an hour or two in daylight to see the place people actually live in, ideally with a local guide or driver. The tradeoff is that it is not a base: lodging is thin, and it is not where you want to be wandering after dark.

NEGRIL

Ninety minutes west, and the honest answer for anyone whose whole trip is the beach — long flat sand at one end, sunset cliffs at the other. Most people day-trip it and regret not sleeping there. The tradeoff is the road: three hours of driving round trip, and you are far from the airport on departure morning.

WHEN TO GO — AND WHEN NOT TO

May into June and September through November are when the rates drop, and the reason is hurricane season, which runs June through November and peaks from August into October. September is the cheapest and the wettest, and some smaller tour operators and restaurants quiet down or close for part of it. If those are your dates, book flexible and buy the coverage. Even in the dry months, expect a brief afternoon shower on the north coast — it passes.

Mid-December through mid-April is the dry, breezy high season, with Christmas and New Year the absolute peak and spring break in March close behind. The one local spike worth marking is the reggae festival Montego Bay hosts every July, which fills rooms across the whole area and pushes rates up in what is otherwise a shoulder month. If you are not coming for the music, move your July dates a week or two either side.

ONE THING TO SKIP

SKIP THE KINGSTON DAY TRIP. Kingston and the Blue Mountains are three to four hours from Montego Bay each way, which means a fourteen-hour day where ten of them are windows and two are the thing you paid for. The same goes for the far eastern parishes. You will arrive tired, see a fraction of a serious city, and spend the next morning recovering. Kingston deserves its own trip flying into the other airport. From Montego Bay, keep the day trips inside a two-hour radius — the west and the near north coast have more than enough, and you will actually be awake for it.
