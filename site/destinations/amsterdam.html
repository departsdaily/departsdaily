KNOW BEFORE YOU LAND — THE TICKET TRAP

Anne Frank House tickets are the whole trip's homework. They are sold online only, released six weeks ahead, and routinely gone within the hour. Set an alarm for the release date before you book anything else. There is no walk-up line anymore — showing up without a ticket gets you a photo of the door.
The Van Gogh Museum is timed entry too. Weekends sell out days ahead in season. The Rijksmuseum is gentler — and cyclists ride straight through the middle of the building via the Museumstraat passage, which tells you most of what you need to know about this city.
The lean is on purpose. Those canal houses tipping toward the street were built that way — the hook on every gable (the hijsbalk) hoists furniture up the outside, and the forward lean keeps your piano off the facade. Four centuries of narrow staircases made it policy.
Stay out of the red asphalt. The smooth reddish lanes are bike lanes, cyclists have right of way, and they will not slow down for a tourist consulting a phone. This is the number one way visitors get hurt here.

DO IT CHEAP — THE PLAYBOOK

The brown café is the point. A bruine kroeg — wood-panel walls stained by a century of smoke, candles on barrels — is where Amsterdam actually happens. In the Jordaan, find one on a canal corner and stay until the second round.
Warm stroopwafels at Albert Cuyp. De Pijp's market street sells them hot off the iron, syrup still melting — a different food from the packaged ones. A few euros, and dinner in De Pijp afterward costs half what the center charges.
Ride the ferries for free. The boats behind Centraal cross the IJ to Noord all day and night at no charge. NDSM wharf on the far side is old shipyards turned studios and waterfront bars.
Vondelpark like a local. Pick up market cheese and bread, claim grass by the pond, and watch the entire city cycle past. The best free afternoon in the Netherlands.
Eat Indonesian once. Amsterdam's colonial history left it the best Indonesian food in Europe — order a rijsttafel, a table-covering parade of small dishes, and skip a museum for it if you must.
