THE BRIEFING — NASSAU STRAIGHT UP

Nassau is a port town that happens to have beaches attached, and the trip gets better the moment you plan around that. The cruise wharf sits at the top of Bay Street, right in the middle of the old colonial grid, and when several ships are berthed at once the same three or four blocks go from empty to shoulder-to-shoulder before lunch and back to empty by dinner. Nothing about the city is broken. It just runs on a schedule most visitors never bother to look up.

New Providence is small — roughly twenty miles end to end, driveable in under an hour — so the island is really Nassau town, the resort strips west and across the bridge, and residential everything else. It suits people who want warm water without a long-haul flight, first-time international travellers, and anyone happy to alternate a beach day with a boat day. It does not suit anyone picturing an empty Out Island. The postcard emptiness — the sandbars, the swimming pigs, the water with nothing in it — lives in the Exumas, which is a boat charter or a short hop away, not a walk down the beach.

One detail that matters more to a Charlotte traveller than any of the guidebook stuff: Nassau has U.S. Customs and Border Protection preclearance. You clear U.S. immigration in the terminal before you board, which means you land back home as a domestic arrival and walk straight out with your bag. It also means you need to be at the airport earlier than a two-and-a-half-hour flight suggests. And the Bahamas drives on the left, which is the single best argument for skipping the rental car.

WHERE YOU'D ACTUALLY BE STANDING

DOWNTOWN / BAY STREET

The old town: pastel government buildings around Parliament Square, the straw market, the wharf, and the limestone steps cut below Fort Fincastle. Cheapest beds on the island and walkable to the Paradise Island ferry. The tradeoff is rhythm — it is loud and crowded when ships are in, and genuinely dead after they sail, with much of it shuttered on Sundays.

CABLE BEACH

The resort strip a few miles west of town, with the island's most reliable swimming beach out front and a jitney or taxi ride back into Nassau. Good middle ground if you want sand at the door without Paradise Island rates. The tradeoff is that you will eat inside the resort orbit most nights unless you deliberately drive out of it.

PARADISE ISLAND

Across the bridge, dominated by the Atlantis complex, with calmer beaches at the eastern end and the marina in between. Fits families who are already paying for waterpark days and want the walk to be short. The tradeoff is that it is an island of resorts priced on the assumption you will not leave — and leaving means a toll or a ferry.

EAST NASSAU / FORT MONTAGU

The residential eastern shore, built around an eighteenth-century fort with a small, shallow, locals' beach beside it. This is where you stay on a second visit, when you would rather be around Bahamians than lanyards. The tradeoff is real: no nightlife, few restaurants within walking distance, and a taxi habit you should budget for.

WHEN TO GO — AND WHEN NOT TO

Cheap runs mid-May to early December, and the floor is August through October. That overlaps hurricane season, which officially runs June 1 to November 30 and peaks from mid-August into October — so take the refundable room and the insurance seriously rather than as an upsell. Summer days are hot and humid with short, violent afternoon storms that pass in an hour, and the water is at its warmest. Late April, May and November are the sweet spot: dry-season weather at wet-season prices.

Expensive starts mid-December and holds through April, which is the dry season and the reason winter rates are what they are. Christmas and New Year are the hardest week of the year to book, because Junkanoo — the costume-and-brass parade that defines the Bahamian calendar — takes over Bay Street in the small hours of Boxing Day and again on New Year's Day, starting well before dawn. March spring break and Easter week both spike rooms and boat charters.

ONE THING TO SKIP

SKIP DOWNTOWN ON SHIP DAYS. Do not build your downtown day around the middle of a day with several ships in port. The market stalls and Bay Street shops price and queue for that crowd, the tour touts work harder, and the walk up to the fort turns into a line. Cruise arrival schedules for the port are published and free to look up the night before — thirty seconds of homework. Go downtown at eight in the morning and be finished by eleven, or pick a day with nothing berthed. On a no-ship day the same streets are pleasant and the same vendors will actually have a conversation with you.
